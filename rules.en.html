01The Job

In the far future the inhabited planets have all but exhausted their mineral wealth. Prospectors are the spacefarers who push out into the empty deep to find raw material and dig it out.

Each player is one such prospector. Over the course of the game you try to claim as many valuable resources as you can and haul them back to your home base. Along the way your ship must be rebuilt, upgraded and tuned to your strategy. Careful navigation and clever use of scarce fuel are what keep you a nose ahead of the other players.

But take care — not every encounter out here is friendly. Hard-won cargo can be lost in moments to an aggressive rival.

03The Board

The board has an inner region and an outer region.

  • The inner region is made of black cells plus the coloured home bases of the ships. This is where ships actually fly.
  • The outer region is made of grey cells and exists to orient ship movement — a ship that ends its drift here has left the playable field and must correct.
  • Around the field are holding areas for resources, booster cards, fuel cards and equipment cards, plus printed reference text and diagrams for key rules.
  • At the centre of the board is a single golden cell, used as the origin when rolling resources onto the board.

Geometry (Phase 0–1)

A hexagon of pointy-top cells on axial coordinates centred on the golden origin. Inner region: distance 0–9 (nine rings). Outer region: distance 10–11 (two rings). A drift ending beyond distance 11 loses the ship. The board hexagon has flat top/bottom edges and W/E vertices.

Six home bases, one per ship colour, sit at the hexagon's six corners inside the inner region (anchored around distance 9), each a 4-cell cluster. Clockwise from West: black W, red NW, blue NE, white E, green SE, yellow SW — opposite colours on opposite corners. Each base points toward its own colour's die direction (see 5).

Dev note · open

The exact cells of each 4-cluster are generated deterministically by board/build_board.py and verified against the physical board's render.

04Setup

  1. Place the resource tiles in the board's resource supply area. Equal numbers of each colour are played, scaled to the player count: 2 players 5×3, 3 players 6×3, 4 players 7×3, 5 players 8×3, 6 players 9×3 (that many of each colour). Tiles not played are returned to the box.
  2. Shuffle the booster cards (star on the back) and place them face down on the booster field.
  3. Shuffle the equipment cards (cone on the back). Deal 3 to each player; each keeps 1 and returns the other 2. Reshuffle the returned and remaining cards and place them face down on the equipment field.
  4. Each player draws one of the 6 ship cards face down and places their ship (3 cones stacked) on a cell of the matching-colour home base.
  5. Each player takes fuel cards equal to the fuel-tank count printed on their ship card. 10 = 2×3 + 4×1. 13 = 3×3 + 4×1.
  6. Place six value-3 fuel cards on the board's fuel field.
  7. Roll one green resource onto the board for each player (see Seeding Resources).
  8. Roll one additional green resource onto the board.
  9. Determine the start player by rolling.
  10. The start player takes the first turn.

Game length

The number of resources put into play sets the length of the game. For a shorter, luckier game, reduce the count by 1 resource per colour. For a longer, more strategic game, increase it by 1 per colour.

05Seeding Resources

Resources enter play at the start of the game and whenever a player delivers resources to a home base. The rule: there must always be exactly one more resource in play than the game's starting player count. A resource is “in play” if it is on the board or loaded as cargo on a ship card. The count uses the initial player number — it does not change when a player is eliminated — and seeding only ever adds resources, guaranteeing there is always something to chase.

The 3 coordinate dice are one 1-pip die, one 2-pip die and one 3-pip die; every face carries one of the six ship/base colours. A roll gives three vectors of length 1, 2 and 3, each pointing toward that colour's home-base corner from the golden centre; add them to get the target cell. The order the dice are read in does not matter.

If the target cell is occupied, roll again until a free cell is found. Bring resources into play in colour order: green first, then yellow, then red.

06Ship Cards

Each player is dealt one ship card in setup. It shows a ship of one colour and that ship's starting equipment, given as six symbols each with a number.

The six equipment ratings and what they govern:

  • Shields — defence strength in combat.
  • Lasers — attack strength in combat.
  • Fuel tanks — how many fuel cards the player holds.
  • Cargo holds — how many resources the ship can carry.
  • Engines — the ship's acceleration capacity (cells burnable per turn).
  • Booster capacity — how many booster cards the player may hold in hand.

07Anatomy of a Turn

  1. Draw a booster card face down from the booster deck into your hand. If this puts you over your hand limit (your booster capacity), immediately play or discard one booster card face up to the booster discard pile.
  2. Move your ship — the drift phase, then the burn phase (see Movement).
  3. After moving, you may either load one resource or attack one player — never both in the same turn.

Alternative: at the very start of your turn, before drawing a booster card, you may scrap your ship — give it up and place it, empty of cargo, in your home base. Your turn then ends.

08Movement: Drift & Burn

Ship movement imitates flight in space. It has two phases: drift, then burn.

On the field a ship is shown with its 3 cones: two cones stacked mark the current position, and one cone marks the previous position. When a ship is at rest, all three cones are stacked and the drift phase is skipped.

Drift

The ship has coasted since your last turn at constant speed and heading. Carry that momentum forward:

  1. Lift the top cone off the current position and place it so that the previous-position cone, the remaining current cone and the new cone lie in a straight line, with the new cone the same distance from the remaining cone as the previous-position cone is. In vector terms: new = current + (current − previous).
  2. Then move the previous-position cone onto the new cone.

The current position has now shifted, and the old current position becomes the new previous position.

  • If the new current position is not a free inner-field cell, this must be corrected by the following burn.
  • If the new current position is outside the outer field, the ship is lost — even if a burn could still reach a valid cell.

Burn

After drifting, you may use engines and fuel to change speed and/or heading. This is mandatory if the ship did not land on a free inner-field cell after drift.

  • Move the current position (the 2 stacked cones) in any direction.
  • Each cell of movement costs one fuel tank, flipped from full (green) to empty (red).
  • The maximum number of cells is your engine rating. Engine boosters can raise it. Absolute maximum burn is 3 cells.
  • The destination cell must be free.
  • Spending 4 engines and 4 fuel performs a hyperspace jump.

On leaving a home base, the ship may burn one extra cell for free, without spending engines or fuel.

10Loading Resources

If your ship ends its movement on a cell directly adjacent to a resource, you may load that resource. Loaded resources are placed on your ship card.

  • Loaded resources may not exceed your cargo hold count.
  • If all holds are full and an adjacent resource is more valuable than one you carry, you may swap: load the more valuable one and place the less valuable one on the board on the cell your ship occupies.
  • At most one resource may be loaded per turn.
  • If you loaded a resource this turn, you may not start combat this turn.

11Equipment Cards

Equipment cards permanently improve a ship's ratings. Each time you bring one or more resources to your home base, draw 3 equipment cards, choose 1, and place it face up beside your ship card. Only one equipment card per delivery, regardless of how many resources you delivered.

If you draw 3 identical cards and don't want that upgrade — or may not take it because the rating is already at its maximum — you may draw 3 new cards. Rejected cards go under the deck.

Choosing a fuel-tank equipment card also grants you one value-3 fuel card for your fuel supply.

12Hyperspace

A ship jumps through hyperspace either by playing a Hyperspace booster card or by spending 4 engines and 4 fuel during the burn phase.

  • Remove the ship from the board. Roll the coordinate dice exactly as for seeding resources to determine a destination cell.
  • Place the ship at rest (all 3 cones stacked) on that cell.
  • If the destination cell is occupied, the ship is lost (see Losing Your Ship).

A Hyperspace booster may be played before the burn phase of your turn, or defensively in combat. If played before the burn phase, you may still perform a burn after the destination is determined.

13Combat

If your ship ends its movement directly adjacent to the current position (2 stacked cones) of another player's ship, you may attack it.

To attack, the target ship must have at least 1 resource loaded — there is no point attacking a ship with nothing to steal. The attacker does not need any resource loaded, but must have more cargo holds than resources it currently carries (room for the spoils).

Sequence — follow this order exactly

  1. The attacker may play any number of Laser boosters to raise attack strength. A Laser +50 booster may only be played as a single card.
  2. The defender may play Shield boosters to raise defence, or play a Hyperspace booster to flee. A Shield +99 booster or a Hyperspace booster means no dice are rolled and the attack fails.
  3. Attacker and defender roll the attack die and defence die respectively. If
    lasers (ship + boosters) + attack roll > shields (ship + boosters) + defence roll
    the attacker wins and takes the loser's most valuable loaded resource, placing it as cargo on the winning ship.
  • If an attack fails, the victorious defender may continue as attacker (counterattack).
  • Combat ends when an attack succeeds, or when the victorious defender declines to counterattack.
  • Only one player may be attacked per turn.
  • If combat took place this turn, you may not load a resource this turn.

14Home Base

Each player has a home base of 4 cells in their colour at the edge of the field. You start there and must return delivered resources there.

  • Departing: place the ship on any cell of the base. On leaving, the ship gets one extra free burn cell (no engines or fuel spent).
  • Arriving: reaching any base cell at the end of the movement phase automatically brings the ship to rest (all 3 cones stacked).
  • Refuel: reaching your home base lets you refill your fuel tanks — flip all your fuel cards green side up.
  • Deliver: bringing one or more resources home lets you choose one equipment card. Place the delivered resources on the outer field beside your base. They are now permanently yours — safe, scored, and un-raidable.
  • For each resource delivered home, a new resource is rolled into play from the supply, as long as the supply still holds any.

15Losing Your Ship

A ship is lost when:

  • a hyperspace jump fails (destination occupied);
  • it leaves the outer field during the drift phase;
  • no free inner-field cell can be reached after the burn phase.

When this happens, any resources the ship was carrying are re-rolled into the supply, and the ship is placed at rest in the player's home base.

You may also voluntarily give up your ship at the start of your turn, before drawing a booster card.

If the last resource of the supply has already been distributed, then losing your ship ends the game for you: your ship is removed from the board. You still count the resources you have already delivered when the winner is determined.

16Game End & Scoring

The game ends when all resources from the supply have been delivered to players' home bases.

Each player totals the value of the resources they delivered: green 1, yellow 2, red 3. The player with the highest total wins.

17Worked Example — An Eight-Turn Run

The yellow ship (1 engine) leaves home, collects one resource, and returns it. New players are strongly advised to perform each sub-step — drift step 1, drift step 2, burn — separately, as shown here.

  1. At rest in the home base, so the drift phase is skipped. Leaving the base grants 1 free burn cell, so with 1 fuel the ship burns 2 cells outward.
  2. Drift step 1: place the cone on a line, equal spacing. Drift step 2: bring the old position onto the new one. Then burn 1 cell (1 fuel).
  3. Drift, then burn 1 cell.
  4. Drift, then burn 1 cell — the ship is now adjacent to a resource and loads it.
  5. Drift, then burn 1 cell, heading home.
  6. The player has drawn an Engine +1 booster and plays it: this turn the ship may burn 2 cells (2 fuel).
  7. Drift, then burn 1 cell.
  8. The player plays an Engine +2 booster: this turn the ship may burn 3 cells (3 fuel), reaching the home base. The ship comes to rest and the resource is delivered.

Part II

Design & Architecture for the Digital Revival

The rules above are the target specification for one game — the Prospector mode. This part records the decisions that govern how the digital version (and any future reprint) is built. The project is a movement-engine game system; Prospector is its first and flagship mode.

ABuild Roadmap

Each phase produces something checkable before the next depends on it.

  1. Freeze the ruleset. ✓ Done 2026-09-09. Every open question resolved; rules split into docs/rules-core.md + docs/rules-prospector.md; parameters in config/config.schema.json + config/default.config.json (deck totals verified 45 / 36 / 43). Done when: the document describes one unambiguous game.
  2. Model the board as data. Hex coordinate system, inner/outer boundary, golden origin, six 4-cell home bases, and the die-colour→direction map — expressed as board.json, with an SVG renderer that draws purely from that file. Support named per-mode overlays that mark special cells. Done when: the board renders from data and the dice-direction rule is pinned down.
  3. Model the content as data. Per-mode content packs plus one shared components pack (dice, cones, generic tokens). A validator checks every count against the rulebook. Done when: every component in the box exists as a data row that validates.
  4. Headless rules engine. ✓ Done 2026-09-09. TypeScript + Vitest under engine/: hex math, seeded RNG, board model, dice, drift/burn/hyperspace, decks, combat, the applyAction reducer + legalActions, simulate() with bots. 61 tests green; the worked example replays move-for-move. Done when: the test suite passes and the example replays move-for-move.
  5. Local hot-seat UI. ✓ First build 2026-09-09. Vite + React over the engine (web/): SVG board from board.json, 3-cone ships with velocity lines, stat tiles / fuel track / typed booster cards, an action bar built entirely from legalActions, click-to-target burns and loads, and a pass-the-device gate. npm run dev. Done when: a full game is playable start to finish by two people at one keyboard.
  6. Movement feel & the component kit. ✓ Done 2026-09-09. web/components/kit.tsx (Cone / FuelTrack / TileChip / Die / BoosterCardFace); a drift preview (ghost of the coast-to cell + projected vector, shown before committing); burn-hover path + fuel-cost badge; cone transform tween and chip settle, both prefers-reduced-motion-gated; combat dice surfaced. Animation whitelist in decisions.md S5. Done when: a new player understands drift without reading section 8.
  7. Multiplayer (optional, later). Move the engine behind an authoritative server; add rooms, turn sync, reconnection. Transport work, not redesign — the engine is already the rules authority. Done when: two machines can play one synced game.
  8. AI opponent (optional). A heuristic bot on top of the engine's legal-move API. Done when: the bot finishes a game making only legal, non-random-looking moves.

Threads that run through every phase: the rulebook is the single source of truth — decide there first, then code; the engine never imports the UI; the eight-turn example stays a permanent regression test; board layout and deck contents live in data files; and every rule and every mode must pass the Physical-Parity Gate.

CSimulation & Balancing

The simulate() function from phase 3 runs thousands of games with scripted bots (random, greedy, greedy + combat), sweeping parameters per mode. It reports:

  • Fairness (hard constraint) — win-rate by ship and by seat position; target is roughly uniform.
  • Balance & feel — game-length distribution, lead volatility and comeback frequency, kingmaking incidents, resource-delivery rate.
  • Mechanic usage — ship-loss rate, hyperspace rate, combat-initiation rate, the impact of a drawn +99 / +50.
  • Skill vs variance — greedy should beat random, and greedy + combat should beat greedy, by a sensible margin. If a random bot wins about 1/N at an N-player table, there is too much luck.
Dev note · tuning the ships

All six starting vectors are the designer's originals (see 6), so the simulator's job here is checking, not designing: if win-rate by ship is not roughly flat, perturb the roster and re-run. A tuned roster overrides modes.prospector.ships; the 2006 values stay as the documented baseline.

Balancing is per-mode; there is no cross-mode fairness to reconcile.

DModes as Plugins

The momentum engine is the game. The goal layer is data plus a few functions.

Core — identical in every variant

  • hex board and coordinate system;
  • the drift / burn momentum engine (3 cones, new = current + (current − previous), fuel-per-cell burn, engine cap, hyperspace);
  • fuel economy (tanks, flip green→red, refuel points);
  • turn skeleton: optional draw → move → optional post-move action;
  • generic card framework (deck, hand, hand-limit, discard, reshuffle);
  • the ship stat vector and the upgrade mechanism;
  • movement-based loss conditions (left the field, no landing cell).

A mode module implements

  • setup() — start positions, decks in play, initial board overlay
  • boardOverlay — which cells are special (bases, start line, finish, checkpoints, hazards)
  • postMoveActions(state) — collect / attack / tag checkpoint / claim / pass
  • checkEnd(state) and score(state)
  • movementInputs(ship) — maps the mode's stats onto what the engine consumes (see E)

FPhysical-Parity Gate

Every rule and every mode must pass this before it is accepted:

  1. All randomness comes from real dice or shuffled decks — never “roll, then apply a formula the player cannot do in their head.”
  2. All state is visible or hand-holdable: cones, cards, tiles in tracks. Nothing that needs a running tally a person would want a spreadsheet for.
  3. Every count parameter resolves to a fixed, printable component set for each player count. The simulator tunes within what fits in a box.
  4. Strict turn order; no real-time or simultaneous resolution.
  5. If the digital build auto-highlights legal moves, the printed rulebook gives a hand-followable procedure that produces the same answer — this mostly bites the drift construction and adjacency.
  6. Hidden information is limited to hand-of-cards and face-down draws — things a card back and a screen can both do.
  7. Misplays are take-backs; no hidden irreversible trigger.

This constraint and the parameterisation of B / C pull against each other in a healthy way: the simulator explores the space, the parity gate bounds it to buildable configurations.

GVisual Language: Physical Objects Only

The renderer is a small kit of real-component widgets — Card, Chip, Die, Cone, Tile, Track — and every mode's screen is composed from it. That mirrors the core / module split on the visual side.

  • Every on-screen element corresponds to something that would be in the box.
  • Every animation is a rigid-body motion of a physical object: a card flips, a die tumbles and settles, a chip slides, a cone is lifted and set down. No HUD, health bars, floating numbers, particle effects, or sci-fi glass UI.
  • “If you can't do it to a cardboard chip, don't animate it.” Short durations; respects prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Card fronts and backs, tile art and token art are the same assets a reprint needs — drawn once, used in both.

This makes the digital build double as a “how to play” demonstrator for the physical game, and it applies the parity gate at the presentation layer.
